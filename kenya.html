Our home water. Coral drop-offs off Diani, the historic wrecks of the Lamu archipelago, and diveable, bath-warm sea every month of the year.

The place

Home base,
and the easiest way in.

Deep Frontier is based in Nairobi, and the Kenyan coast is where most of our divers get their first taste of warm-water technical diving. The Indian Ocean here is diveable all year, the reefs run from gentle recreational shallows to deep offshore drop-offs, and the logistics — flights, fills, boats — are the simplest of anywhere we operate.

The south coast around Diani is the training and day-boat hub: fringing reef, walls and abundant fish life within a short run. North, the Lamu archipelago trades convenience for wildness — remote Swahili coast, old trade-route wrecks, and reefs that see very few divers. In between sit protected marine parks and, in season, whale sharks and mantas moving along the coast.

Where you'll dive

The water,
region by region.

The Kenyan coast isn't one dive site — it's a whole coastline, from the day-boat reefs off Diani to remote wrecks in the far north, plus a deep freshwater crater lake inland. Here's what each stretch actually offers.

Diani & the south coast

Reef · wreck · 12–40 m

Our training and day-boat hub. Short runs from the beach reach fringing reef, coral walls and offshore pinnacles, and the coast's best-known wreck — the MV Funguo, a fishing vessel scuttled as an artificial reef, sitting upright at around 30 m and now thick with glassfish and soft coral. Further south, the protected reefs of Kisite-Mpunguti hold dolphins, turtles and dense fish life on the shallower profiles.

MV Funguo wreck · ~30 m Galu offshore reefs · 18–25 m Kisite-Mpunguti Marine Park

Mombasa

Wreck · marine park · to 30 m

The easiest coast to reach and a solid wreck day out. The MV Dania, a cargo ship sunk as an artificial reef, sits in around 20–30 m with resident groupers, scorpionfish and honeycomb morays through the holds. The shallower reefs of Mombasa Marine National Park make a relaxed second dive and a good check-out or refresher site.

MV Dania wreck · 20–30 m Mombasa Marine Park reefs

Watamu

Reef · canyons · big animals

One of Kenya's oldest protected reefs — a marine park gazetted in 1968 — and some of the most varied topography on the coast. Coral canyons, overhangs and bommies at The Canyon, Moray Reef and Brain Coral, with the outer sites dropping past 30 m. In season the same waters draw whale sharks and mantas moving down the coast.

The Canyon Moray Reef Brain Coral · Drummers Whale shark season

Lamu archipelago

Remote · wrecks · exploratory

The far north, and the wildest diving we run. Remote Swahili coast with old trade-route and colonial-era wrecks, untouched offshore reefs around Manda Toto, and stretches of water that see almost no other divers. Genuine exploratory potential for those who want to be first down a site rather than following a mooring line.

Manda Toto reefs Historic trade-route wrecks Exploratory offshore

Lake Chala

Altitude · crater walls · to 90 m+

Something completely different, inland on the Kenya–Tanzania border below Kilimanjaro. Chala is a steep-walled volcanic crater lake — clear, cool freshwater over submerged rock walls that drop beyond 90 m. It's a proper technical and altitude site, a world away from the reefs, and pairs perfectly with an overland leg between the coast and the mountains.

Crater wall · beyond 90 m Altitude / freshwater Kenya–Tanzania border
